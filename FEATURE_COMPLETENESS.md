# Repeated feature components: release checklist

A feature draft may exist before a user supplies a photo. Do not describe a published visual component as complete merely because its text renders. Research readiness and visual completeness are separate.

For every published artist/producer card, compare the full component beside its peers at 320, 390, 768 and 1280 CSS pixels, including the archive and homepage placements. Verify:

- The supplied photo, when available, is the exact approved full composition, contained at its natural aspect ratio without face cropping or extra overlays.
- The name graffiti is a real approved transparent image, not a forgotten typography placeholder. All lettering and opaque white paint remain visible. A generation refusal is a blocker to disclose, never a reason to bypass safeguards.
- The name image has its own contained row, with consistent spacing and complete painted bounds. Equal card height must not be achieved by excessive image letterboxing.
- The visible name, accessible name, photo attribution, source disclosure and original-art credit are truthful and do not duplicate announcements unnecessarily.
- Canonical and legacy routes, SPA and no-JS output, metadata, OGP image and sitemap entries agree. Existing published images and editorial content remain protected.
- Buttons, menus, focus outlines and floating controls do not cover image paint, text or neighboring actions. Capture the entire component, not only the inner image/link.
- Actions remain available by keyboard and assistive technology even when touch presentation favors gestures. All action targets remain usable at small widths.

Run machine checks for exact asset hashes, decoded dimensions/alpha, complete IDs, image-to-container geometry, source/OGP references and state preservation. Then inspect actual rendered whole-component captures. Bounding boxes alone do not establish visibility when a sibling overlays the image.

Visual consistency does not mean duplicated editorial language. Each feature must retain a distinct, source-grounded argument and its own factual citations.
